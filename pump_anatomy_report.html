Anatomy of a pump

561 pumps (+30% or more within 24h) on Bybit USDT perpetuals, last 90 days. Each one is lined up at its start (the low before the run, hour 0) and followed from 48 hours before to 24 hours after. The blue line is the typical (median) pump; the gray band is how the same indicator looks at normal times on the same coins.

What it shows

  • Volume and volatility build up quietly for a day or two before the start (about 1.2–1.5× normal vs 0.9 at normal times), then explode after it.
  • Before the start, traders lean short: the long/short account ratio is lower than normal (≈1.55 vs 1.87), and open interest creeps up. Part of many pumps is a short squeeze.
  • Price usually dips just before the start (RSI ≈37). Partly by design: hour 0 is the low point, so a dip into it is built in.
  • BTC does nothing special. These pumps are coin-specific.
  • None of it is a reliable warning. Each sign is "unusual" before only 20–27% of pumps, against 10% at normal times: about 2–2.5× more often, and far from a trigger you could trade alone.

How it was made: 15-minute Bybit candles, 388 coins. "Normal times" = random moments on the same coins at least 3 days away from any pump. Volume and candle size are measured against each coin's typical level in the week ending 48 hours before, so the pump can't inflate its own baseline. Futures data (open interest, funding, long/short) covers only the last ~30 days, so those charts use about 200 pumps. Research, not financial advice.
